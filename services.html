Sports Injury Massage

As a Level 5 qualified Sports and Remedial Massage Therapist, my approach goes far beyond a standard sports massage. "Remedial" means we actively address, treat, and rehabilitate specific musculoskeletal issues, persistent pain, and movement dysfunction, rather than simply soothing sore muscles.

Every session begins with a clinical assessment and case history to identify the root cause of your discomfort. To address these specific issues, I utilize advanced manual therapy techniques, including:

  • Soft Tissue Release (STR)
  • Muscle Energy Techniques (MET)
  • Trigger-Point Therapy
  • Myofascial Release
  • Joint Mobilisation

By combining these targeted clinical soft-tissue techniques with my extensive background in movement coaching, I provide a comprehensive treatment that not only provides immediate symptom relief but actively restores optimal movement, function, and long-term resilience.

A Modern Approach to Movement Rehabilitation

My work combines multiple evidence-based disciplines and rehabilitation methods to create highly personalised movement and recovery sessions tailored to the individual rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

These methods include:

  • Biomechanics & Gait Analysis
  • Anatomy in Motion (AiM)
  • Strength & Conditioning principles
  • Progressive and eccentric loading strategies
  • Neuromuscular re-education
  • Proprioception and kinaesthetic awareness training
  • Overcoming isometrics
  • Movement coaching and tactile cueing techniques

Rather than relying on rigid rehabilitation protocols or generic exercise plans, I adapt sessions to the individual in front of me. Every person moves differently, learns differently, and responds differently to movement coaching and rehabilitation.

Many people struggle to learn movement effectively through standard demonstrations, videos, or repetitive exercise prescriptions alone. My approach uses intuitive tactile feedback and movement awareness coaching to help clients better understand how their body moves, compensates, and responds to load.

The goal is not simply short-term symptom relief, but helping the body develop more efficient, adaptable, and resilient movement strategies that support long-term confidence and physical performance.

This approach also supports athletes and active individuals looking to optimise biomechanics, improve performance, increase mobility and strength, and reduce the likelihood of recurring injuries through more efficient movement patterns.

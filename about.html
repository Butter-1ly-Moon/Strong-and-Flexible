My Philosophy, A Search For Answers

My approach is born from necessity.

Before becoming a movement coach and rehabilitation specialist, I spent years pushing my body through demanding physical pursuits. I competed in skydiving, served in the military as a paratrooper, trained in martial arts, climbed, ran ultra-marathons and, like many highly active people, accumulated more than my fair share of injuries along the way.

As I moved through different rehabilitation systems, I became increasingly frustrated by what I was seeing. Many approaches seemed focused on managing symptoms rather than understanding why the problem existed in the first place.

That frustration sparked a long-term search for better answers.

I immersed myself in different disciplines, studying Pilates, Osteopathic techniques, Yoga and Clinical Massage while continually searching for a deeper understanding of movement and recovery. Eventually, I discovered Anatomy in Motion (AiM), a methodology developed by Gary Ward that transformed the way I viewed human movement. It helped me understand that the root cause of many issues may not be where symptoms appear. Instead, the answer often lies in how the body moves as a connected system, starting with the feet and gait mechanics.

Why Strong And Flexible Exists

The Gap In The Industry

I created Strong and Flexible because the rehab and fitness industry is trapped in silos. You will find manual therapists, osteopaths, and chiropractors with amazing palpation skills who don't have a clue about dynamic movement mechanics. You will find excellent Pilates or yoga teachers who don't understand heavy strength training, load management, or endurance. And you have high-level S&C or grappling coaches who don't understand joint biomechanics.

My Personal Story

If you look at me today, at 46 years old, doing splits, backbends, and handstands, and regularly sparring in MMA and BJJ with guys half my age, people assume I was born with an elite athletic background. The truth is the exact opposite. I was an undiagnosed autistic and ADHD kid ...

One of the biggest lessons I learned throughout my own journey is that movement cannot always be reduced to a checklist of exercises or a standard protocol.

Every individual has their own movement history, compensations, habits and physical experiences.

The Clients I Most Enjoy Helping

I am most passionate about working with curious, ambitious, and highly committed individuals. The severity of their past injuries doesn't deter me; in fact, the more complex the history, the better.

True rehabilitation is teamwork. I get excited when a client is open-minded enough to finally feel what optimal mechanics actually are. When they experience that moment of feeling their foot flatten, their knee bend, and their upper back extend simultaneously just to shift their weight efficiently, that is when real change happens.

My most meaningful success story isn't a professional athlete; it's a 73-year-old retired teacher. After two years of consistent, committed teamwork, she is now executing 20kg Jefferson Curls for sets of 10 and routinely performs split squats, back extensions and reverse hypers.

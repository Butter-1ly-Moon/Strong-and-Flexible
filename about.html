My Philosophy, A Search For Answers

My approach is born from necessity.

Before becoming a movement coach and rehabilitation specialist, I spent years pushing my body through demanding physical pursuits. I competed in skydiving, served in the military as a paratrooper, trained in martial arts, climbed, ran ultra-marathons and, like many highly active people, accumulated more than my fair share of injuries along the way.

As I moved through different rehabilitation systems, I became increasingly frustrated by what I was seeing. Many approaches seemed focused on managing symptoms rather than understanding why the problem existed in the first place.

That frustration sparked a long-term search for better answers.

I immersed myself in different disciplines, studying Pilates, Osteopathic techniques, Yoga and Clinical Massage while continually searching for a deeper understanding of movement and recovery.

Eventually, I discovered Anatomy in Motion (AiM), a methodology developed by Gary Ward that transformed the way I viewed human movement. It helped me understand that the root cause of many issues may not be where symptoms appear. Instead, the answer often lies in how the body moves as a connected system, starting with the feet and gait mechanics.

That discovery continues to shape the way I work today.

Why Strong And Flexible Exists

The Gap In The Industry

I created Strong and Flexible because the rehab and fitness industry is trapped in silos. You will find manual therapists, osteopaths, and chiropractors with amazing palpation skills who don't have a clue about dynamic movement mechanics. You will find excellent Pilates or yoga teachers who don't understand heavy strength training, load management, or endurance. And you have high-level S&C or grappling coaches who don't understand joint biomechanics.

Strong and Flexible exists to combine all of these disciplines. I bridge the gap between deep biomechanics, manual therapy, and heavy strength and conditioning.

My Personal Story, The 'Why'

If you look at me today, at 46 years old, doing splits, backbends, and handstands, and regularly sparring in MMA and BJJ with guys half my age, people assume I was born with an elite athletic background. The truth is the exact opposite.

I was an undiagnosed autistic and ADHD kid. I was fussy, highly sensitive, constantly injured, and always in pain. Because of my hypermobility, I was constantly spraining my ankles. I was not a natural athlete. But despite my perceived genetic disadvantages, I had an obsessive curiosity about how the human body works and how it heals.

Instead of accepting a life of pain, I reverse-engineered my own body. I developed deep proprioception and mechanical awareness to turn my disadvantages into an advantage. That curiosity allowed me to become a paratrooper in an elite Polish airborne unit, logging over 500 jumps. It allowed me to rock climb, run ultramarathons, and now train MMA. I am literally getting better and more capable as I age.

I built Strong and Flexible to teach people the exact same thing: how to become fiercely aware of their own body, rewire their mechanics, and realize that a perceived physical disadvantage is just a problem waiting to be solved.

The Biggest Misconception

The most dangerous misconception in the fitness and rehab industry is confusing interoception with proprioception. People assume that because they can intensely 'feel' their body, they have good body awareness. They are completely wrong.

These two senses operate on entirely different neural pathways. Interoception is your ability to feel internal sensations, like a deep stretch or muscle fatigue. Proprioception is your mechanical awareness of where your body is in three-dimensional space, such as exactly where your weight is distributed over your feet.

I see this constantly with highly sensitive individuals, hypermobile clients, dancers, and even very experienced yoga teachers. They chase extreme stretches to 'connect with their body' because they are highly interoceptive. They feel a lot, so they think they have elite body awareness. But if you ask them to stand still and tell you if their center of mass is shifted over their right heel or their left toes, they have absolutely no idea. They are mechanically blind, but because they feel sensations so strongly, they think they can see.

I spend most of my time explaining that just because you feel your body doesn't mean you have awareness of it in space. True proprioception is a mechanical skill. It requires training your brain to feel weight distribution and joint position, not just chasing the sensation of a stretched muscle.

I am most passionate about working with curious, ambitious, and highly committed individuals. The severity of their past injuries doesn't deter me, in fact, the more complex the history, the better. My Most Meaningful Success Story

My most meaningful success story isn't a professional athlete; it’s a 73-year-old retired teacher. She came to me initially because she was diagnosed with near end-stage knee osteoarthritis and wanted to avoid a total knee replacement. After two years of consistent, committed teamwork and finding the exact right entry point for her mechanics, she is now executing 20kg Jefferson Curls for sets of 10. She routinely performs split squats, back extensions, and reverse hypers, movements most people half her age are terrified to attempt. We rarely reach the true limits of our physical capacity because we don't know how to systematically find them. With the right progression and commitment, the human body is incredibly adaptable.
